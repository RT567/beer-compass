## The idea (Rob)
> A completely stupid application, mostly for mobile, where it points in the direction of the closest place
> where you can buy beer or just more generally some alcohol… whether or not they're open is important…
> Without telling you how close it is, just which direction, like a compass. Don't overly stylize it.
> It just says beer compass at the top. And then it has the arrow. The arrow should move live.

Rob's later rules (same day): **only the needle on screen** — no title, no credit line ("just a fucking raw
compass"; the tab title stays "Beer Compass"). The OSM credit lives in an HTML comment and this repo instead.
Earlier rules: **no money spent** ("OpenStreetMap and guessing… it's not actually a useful
app"); **only needs to work in Sydney**; and **"It shouldn't say where or what or any information about how
far away the place is. It shouldn't say start. It should just be an arrow and beer compass."** Keep it that way.

## Data
- `scripts/fetch_venues.py` pulls Greater Sydney (bbox -34.25,150.50,-33.40,151.40) from Overpass:
  `amenity=pub|bar|biergarten|nightclub`, `shop=alcohol|wine`, `craft=brewery`. Writes `data/venues.json`
  (rows `[lat, lon, kind, opening_hours, name]`, ~85 KB). 2026-10-05: 1,456 venues, 330 with opening_hours (23%).
  Re-run by hand now and then; nothing schedules it.
- Overpass gotcha: it answers **406** to curl's default UA and to some fake browser UAs; a descriptive UA works.
  Real browsers get 200 + CORS `*`, but the app doesn't call Overpass at runtime (Sydney-only → static file:
  faster, no rate limits, location never leaves the phone).
- Paid alternatives with much better hours (Google Places Nearby Search Enterprise, 1k free/month; TomTom
  2.5k/day free) were researched and rejected: Rob doesn't want accounts or spend.
- ODbL wants visible attribution. A faint footer credit was there at first; Rob had it removed. The credit is
  now an HTML comment in index.html, in `data/venues.json` (`source`) and in these notes.

## Opening hours (`hours.js`)
- Own ~100-line parser (the opening_hours.js library is 714 KB + suncalc + i18next). Handles day lists/ranges
  incl. wrap-around (Su-Th), multiple time ranges, past-midnight closes, `off`, `24/7`, `,` used as a rule
  separator, later-rule-overrides-day semantics. PH/SH ignored. Parses 324/330 Sydney strings; the rest
  (comments, "late", month rules) return null.
- No/unparseable hours → guessed by kind (`Hours.GUESS`): pub 10–24 (Fr/Sa –01, Su –22), bar 16–24 (Fr/Sa –02),
  nightclub Th–Sa 21–03, bottle shop 9–21 (Su 10–20), brewery We–Su 12–22, biergarten 12–22.
- Known and guessed hours count equally; the arrow targets the nearest open one (haversine), re-picked on every
  GPS fix and every 30 s. Uses the phone's local time (fine: Sydney only).

## Compass (`app.js`)
- iOS: `deviceorientation` → `webkitCompassHeading` (magnetic). Needs `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission()`
  inside a user gesture: **the first tap anywhere** asks (no button, per Rob). Until then the arrow is faded.
- Android Chrome: `deviceorientationabsolute`, heading from alpha/beta/gamma using the screen-top (y) axis plus the
  out-of-the-back (-z) axis so it works flat and upright. Firefox: `deviceorientation` with `absolute`.
- Chrome 153 also exposes `requestPermission`, so the "needs a tap" path covers it too.
- Magnetic → true: +12.8° (WMM at Sydney CBD 2026; Penrith 12.6, Cronulla 12.9). Plus `screen.orientation.angle`.
- Location: `watchPosition` starts on load (browser shows the permission pop-up); tap retries if it failed.
- Smoothing: shortest-way-round lerp, 0.15 per frame.

## Look (Rob, later the same day)
> The background should be like a bubbly beer… as though you took a photo of the very side of a beer in a
> see-through glass… same colour, the consistency. It should move a little bit when you move the phone.
> Actually maybe there is no head… just the same consistent beer thing the whole way through.

`beer.js` draws it on a full-screen canvas behind everything: amber vertical gradient, darker at the sides
(round glass), three soft vertical glass reflections, ~W·H/1500 scattered bubbles (mostly tiny, some faint =
"further back", bigger ones rise faster and wobble) plus a few nucleation streams from fixed points at the
bottom. Bubbles rise towards real "up" from `deviceorientation` beta/gamma (world-up in device coords =
(-cosβ·sinγ, sinβ, cosβ·cosγ); flat phone → plain up), and the glass layer slides ±20 px with the tilt. No head.
Title and arrow are white with a soft brown shadow. ~145 fps in desktop Chrome at 390×844@3x.

Streams (Rob: "a few different sources… they should shift a little bit left and right"): 4–8 nucleation
points (W/70), each swaying ±4–16 px at 0.15–0.5 rad/s; each lives 12–30 s, then restarts somewhere else
(its last bubbles just rise away). Stream bubbles meander slightly too.

## Moving the phone (Rob: "if the bubbles could move as you move your phone around, that'd be awesome")
`devicemotion` in beer.js: **slosh** — `acceleration` (no gravity) shoves the liquid the opposite way across
the screen (220 px/s per m/s²·s, capped 600 px/s, decays e^-2.5t); **swirl** — `rotationRate.alpha` (turning the
phone flat, like with a compass) rotates the whole bubble field the other way about the screen centre at 70% of
the turn rate (decays e^-1.5t). Bubbles live in a square field the size of the screen diagonal so swirling never
empties the corners; only on-screen ones are drawn. iOS: app.js also calls `DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission()`
on the first tap (same Motion & Orientation permission). **Unverified on real phones: the sign of iOS
`acceleration`** (historically opposite to Android) — if slosh goes the wrong way on iPhone, flip it there.

## Arrow
Rob didn't like the first arrow (a big solid navigation arrowhead): "quite simple and quite just chill". Three
styles were made, switchable with `?arrow=`: **needle** (slim compass needle, bright end points at the beer,
faded tail, small amber hub), **soft** (the old arrowhead, slimmer, rounded corners), **line** (stroked arrow,
round caps). Rob picked the **needle** ("I think that looks great"); it's the default. The other two stay
behind the flag. Softer drop shadow than before.

## The idiot prompt (`idiot.js`)
Rob: after the browser asks for your location, "a second pop-up asking if you're an idiot… yes, no… and it
just does nothing." Browsers don't allow custom text in real permission prompts, so it's a fake one styled
like the system prompt: an iOS alert ("“rt567.github.io” Would Like to Know If You’re an Idiot", No | Yes) on
iPhone/iPad, a Chrome-style dialog elsewhere; light/dark follow the system. app.js fires a `located` event
once the location prompt is answered (first fix or error; 500 ms after load with `?at=`); the fake prompt
appears 700 ms later. Every answer just closes it, but is **remembered like a real permission answer** (Rob:
"remember the information the same as the location information, so it's believable-ish"): iOS Yes/No kept 24 h
(Safari remembers a site's location answer for a day); Chrome-style offers "Yes, while visiting the site" (kept
for good), "Yes, this time" (sessionStorage, until the tab closes), "No, never" (kept for good) — modelled on
Chrome's "Allow while visiting the site / Allow this time / Never allow". Clear with
`localStorage.removeItem("idiot"); sessionStorage.removeItem("idiot")`. When it's remembered there's no prompt to
tap, so the compass unlock waits for a tap anywhere (arrow faded until then). On iOS the tap bubbles to app.js's
document click handler, so answering it also triggers the real compass permission prompt — intended.
Shows the live host name; on localhost it pretends to be rt567.github.io.

## Android fix (2026-10-05, Rob: "the needle is just stuck pointing to the left-hand side of the screen")
Stuck needle = no headings, so it fell back to "up = north". Chrome 153 has `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission`,
and per the current spec it takes `absolute`: without `true` only the relative sensors are granted, so
`deviceorientationabsolute` never fires. Now `requestPermission(true)`. Fallback if still no heading 1.5 s after
the grant: `AbsoluteOrientationSensor` (Generic Sensor API, referenceFrame "screen"), heading from its quaternion
with the same y−z axis trick. `?debug` shows a second line: permission result, event counts (abs / rel / ios /
gs = generic sensor, with its error), last raw values, heading, screen angle. Not yet confirmed on Rob's phone.

Still stuck after that (Rob: needle stuck left **and the bubbles never move**, so no sensor events at all).
Chrome 151 shipped the DeviceOrientation permission API: "websites that register event listeners will not receive
motion or orientation events until they call requestPermission()" (blink-dev Intent to Ship). v6 changes:
the tap asks for orientation (`requestPermission(true)`, falling back to the plain call if refused) **and**
`DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission()` in the same gesture (motion re-asked after the orientation grant for iOS);
after the grant all sensor listeners in app.js and beer.js are detached and reattached (`sensors-granted` event)
in case listeners attached before the grant never start. `?debug` line starts with `v6` so Rob can tell he has
the new build, then `perm abs:<absolute result> <result> motion <result>`.
