## The idea (Rob)
> A completely stupid application, mostly for mobile, where it points in the direction of the closest place
> where you can buy beer or just more generally some alcohol… whether or not they're open is important…
> Without telling you how close it is, just which direction, like a compass. Don't overly stylize it.
> It just says beer compass at the top. And then it has the arrow. The arrow should move live.

Rob's later rules (same day): **no money spent** ("OpenStreetMap and guessing… it's not actually a useful
app"); **only needs to work in Sydney**; and **"It shouldn't say where or what or any information about how
far away the place is. It shouldn't say start. It should just be an arrow and beer compass."** Keep it that way.

## Data
- `scripts/fetch_venues.py` pulls Greater Sydney (bbox -34.25,150.50,-33.40,151.40) from Overpass:
  `amenity=pub|bar|biergarten|nightclub`, `shop=alcohol|wine`, `craft=brewery`. Writes `data/venues.json`
  (rows `[lat, lon, kind, opening_hours, name]`, ~85 KB). 2026-10-05: 1,456 venues, 330 with opening_hours (23%).
  Re-run by hand now and then; nothing schedules it.
- Overpass gotcha: it answers **406** to curl's default UA and to some fake browser UAs; a descriptive UA works.
  Real browsers get 200 + CORS `*`, but the app doesn't call Overpass at runtime (Sydney-only → static file:
  faster, no rate limits, location never leaves the phone).
- Paid alternatives with much better hours (Google Places Nearby Search Enterprise, 1k free/month; TomTom
  2.5k/day free) were researched and rejected: Rob doesn't want accounts or spend.
- ODbL needs attribution: there's a 9px very light grey "© OpenStreetMap contributors" footer. Rob asked for
  "no other information"; the footer was kept for the licence and flagged to him.

## Opening hours (`hours.js`)
- Own ~100-line parser (the opening_hours.js library is 714 KB + suncalc + i18next). Handles day lists/ranges
  incl. wrap-around (Su-Th), multiple time ranges, past-midnight closes, `off`, `24/7`, `,` used as a rule
  separator, later-rule-overrides-day semantics. PH/SH ignored. Parses 324/330 Sydney strings; the rest
  (comments, "late", month rules) return null.
- No/unparseable hours → guessed by kind (`Hours.GUESS`): pub 10–24 (Fr/Sa –01, Su –22), bar 16–24 (Fr/Sa –02),
  nightclub Th–Sa 21–03, bottle shop 9–21 (Su 10–20), brewery We–Su 12–22, biergarten 12–22.
- Known and guessed hours count equally; the arrow targets the nearest open one (haversine), re-picked on every
  GPS fix and every 30 s. Uses the phone's local time (fine: Sydney only).

## Compass (`app.js`)
- iOS: `deviceorientation` → `webkitCompassHeading` (magnetic). Needs `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission()`
  inside a user gesture: **the first tap anywhere** asks (no button, per Rob). Until then the arrow is faded.
- Android Chrome: `deviceorientationabsolute`, heading from alpha/beta/gamma using the screen-top (y) axis plus the
  out-of-the-back (-z) axis so it works flat and upright. Firefox: `deviceorientation` with `absolute`.
- Chrome 153 also exposes `requestPermission`, so the "needs a tap" path covers it too.
- Magnetic → true: +12.8° (WMM at Sydney CBD 2026; Penrith 12.6, Cronulla 12.9). Plus `screen.orientation.angle`.
- Location: `watchPosition` starts on load (browser shows the permission pop-up); tap retries if it failed.
- Smoothing: shortest-way-round lerp, 0.15 per frame.

## Look (Rob, later the same day)
> The background should be like a bubbly beer… as though you took a photo of the very side of a beer in a
> see-through glass… same colour, the consistency. It should move a little bit when you move the phone.
> Actually maybe there is no head… just the same consistent beer thing the whole way through.

`beer.js` draws it on a full-screen canvas behind everything: amber vertical gradient, darker at the sides
(round glass), three soft vertical glass reflections, ~W·H/1500 scattered bubbles (mostly tiny, some faint =
"further back", bigger ones rise faster and wobble) plus a few nucleation streams from fixed points at the
bottom. Bubbles rise towards real "up" from `deviceorientation` beta/gamma (world-up in device coords =
(-cosβ·sinγ, sinβ, cosβ·cosγ); flat phone → plain up), and the glass layer slides ±20 px with the tilt. No head.
Title and arrow are white with a soft brown shadow. ~145 fps in desktop Chrome at 390×844@3x.

## Testing flags
- `?at=-33.8688,151.2093` fakes the position; `?debug` adds a status line (target name, kind, metres, hours source).
- No compass (laptop): after the tap, up = north; ←/→ turn a pretend heading.
- Verified in Chrome devtools at 390×844 with synthetic `deviceorientationabsolute` events: bearing 52.9° to the
  nearest open bar from Martin Place; facing magnetic east → arrow −49.9°, upright facing magnetic north → 40.1°. ✓
- Not yet tried on a real iPhone/Android.
